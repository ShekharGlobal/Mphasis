2. The regular expression: /\b\w/g

This is the key part.

\b → word boundary
Matches the position where a word starts (between a non-word character and a word character)

\w → word character
Matches letters (a–z, A–Z), digits (0–9), or underscore (_)

/g → global flag
Apply the match to all occurrences, not just the first one

Together, \b\w means:
“the first character of every word”
